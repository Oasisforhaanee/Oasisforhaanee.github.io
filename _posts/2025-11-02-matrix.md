A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers, but it also carries deep meaning as a linear transformation.

## Matrix as Vectors

A vector `(1,1)` can be written as the column vector

$$P=\begin{bmatrix}1\\ 1\end{bmatrix}$$

## Basis Vectors

The standard basis vectors in `2D` are

$$\mathbf{i}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\ 0\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf{j}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ 1\end{bmatrix}$$

Any vector in `2D` can be expressed using these basis vectors, e.g. `(3,2)=3i+2j`.

## Change of Basis

Different pairs can also span `2D` space. For example:

$$\mathbf{a}=\begin{bmatrix}2\\ 0\end{bmatrix},\quad \mathbf{b}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\ 2\end{bmatrix},\quad \begin{bmatrix}3\\ 2\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}$$

So `(1,0)` and `(0,1)` are the **standard basis**, but other linearly independent pairs can also be a basis.

## Row Space and Column Space

The **column space** is the set of all linear combinations of the columns. The **row space** is defined similarly from the rows.

## Rank

The **rank** of a matrix is the dimension of its column space. It tells us how many independent directions the columns actually span.

## Linear Transformation

A matrix represents a **linear transformation**. Applying a matrix `A` to `x` changes the space by rotation, shear, stretch, or projection.
